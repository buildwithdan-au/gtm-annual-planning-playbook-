## 1. Looking at the data available, does "opportunity" in FY22 account ownership look evenly distributed per seller?

**a. Why or why not?**

"Opportunity" in FY22 is poorly distributed. This is apparent for several reasons:

i. Overall composition of Prospects to Existing Customers (Customers) is skewed more towards Prospects, but differences between EMEA (16% Customers) and AMER & APAC (+90% Customers) indicate an uneven approach to driving new growth in the EMEA region.

ii. Overall Opportunity Score is distributed evenly in EMEA and AMER, however the quality of Existing Customer opportunity in APAC is significantly lower. Potentially driving poor behavior or gaps in seller attainment compared to peers if on a global compensation plan.

iii. Overall distribution is poorly matched to an average distribution pattern, with a larger frequency of Territories having very high-score opportunity patches, which gaps are significant in the larger majority and the lower end. This may lead to a barbell effect where there are a proportion of high performers and a larger proportion missing target due to poor Territories. 17% of Territories are +2 std dev away from the Mean vs 10% expected. *(See Slide Deck)*

**b. What are some downsides of having one SAL have more "opportunity" than another? Benefits?**

*Downsides include:*

1. Unequal performance of SALs across regions making it hard to measure performance across the aggregate
2. Motivation and SAL attrition. Poor patches lead to sellers leaving Acme Corp
3. Poor behavior across internal teaming - hoarding of resources and other coaching that may be available in a collaborative environment
4. Masked KPI. Such as PipeGen and Pipe progression. In reality there should be a higher output from better Territories

*Upsides include:*

1. In nascent or fast growing Territories it may be wise to give high-quality Territories if they correspond to the GTM strategy (Eg: PubSec may be fast growing - if there is a focused GTM on that segment, it may warrant those accounts that are representative of that Segment)
2. SAL grading - higher grades will require a larger Fin Plan target and thus the Territory may be reflective of the accounts that have been worked on and built out over time (eg: Strat Accounts)
3. Regional differences - buying power in-region may differ, leading to a natural skew between developed and developing markets

**c. Any noteworthy trends by Region?**

i. See slide deck

ii. Key Takeaways:

1. Overall Opportunity Scores in APAC is lower than EMEA and AMER, and has a tighter spread between top territories and bottom territories
2. AMER has the largest spread between top and bottom Opportunity-scored territories
3. APAC has significantly lower percentage of Customers to Prospects - which may be an indication of a newly established business or a focus on New Business (Logos) rather than growth through account expansion

## 3. Disruption is where an account changes ownership, i.e. FY22 Owner <> FY23 Owner. What's disruption look like from FY22 to FY23?

*(Hint: New Accounts should be excluded, since they don't have relevant FY22 ownership)*

Disruption looks exceptionally high, with +90% of accounts having a change in ownership, resulting in 95% of ARR potentially having a disruption impact also.

**a. What are some downsides of account disruption?**

i. Sales - Loss of relationships built up by a specific SAL

ii. Sales - Latency between account change announcements and pipegen being created for the following FY (Sellers getting up to speed on new Territory)

iii. Sales - Any pipegen created by one SAL is leveraged by another - leading to potential poor behavior in Q4 if sellers know accounts will be disrupted. Brings challenges in accurately comparing any KPIs over a trailing 6-12 month period for SALs (Pipegen, ACV attainment, conversion rates, etc)

iv. Customer Success - Handovers and change in account ownership bring additional complexities or noise rather than driving business outcomes for customers

v. Customer Success - Additional pressure coming from being the stable relationship with the customer when delivering on technical projects/deployments

vi. Customers - perception of Vendor relationship churn or instability if not properly communicated

vii. Customers - Actual slowdown of critical sales, deployment or value realisation cycles while they learn to work with new point of contact

**b. Benefits?**

i. Sales - Better resource allocation. Having the right number or grade of seller against a specific territory may mean more time for that seller to focus on a smaller number of customers or prospects

ii. Sales - Performance. Putting higher performing sellers on high-opportunity accounts may result in a larger longer-term growth trajectory. In addition, moving low-performing sellers out of the business will require coverage or new seller's aligned to that territory or account.

iii. Sales - GTM alignment. Having sellers that are aligned to a specific industry or geo may require territory/account change in order to realise benefits

iv. Customers - new relationship may unlock partnering benefits (either skill-based or personality match)

v. Customers - strategic alignment. Having a Strategic Account SAL on an account may signal the importance of that org to a vendor and higher ARR growth opportunities

## 4. Imagine we went ahead and assigned out the proposed FY23 Territories.

**a. What changes would you expect?**

i. More equitable territories will bring a higher average attainment per SAL. While ARR may not increase in unrealistic ways, I would expect more SALs to hit their annual quota attainment

ii. More equitable territories will encourage SALs to collaborate as their opportunities are more similar (in size and quality initially)

iii. High rates of disruption will slow down pipegen in the short term (Q1 after rollout) which may impact H1, dependent on the average deal size/conversion rate/deal cycle length

iv. High rates of disruption will dent morale for higher performers with successful territories. Expect some seller attrition without proper communication, measurement and Sales Leader coaching

v. High rates of disruption may impact Q1 opportunities that had been built up through Q3/Q4 of the previous FY as negotiations and deal close becomes more complex with a change in SAL

vi. For the Regional Sales Leader, I would expect to need to spend time with Partners and Customer Success to ensure changes are well communicated

**b. How would you determine if the new territories were or were not successful?**

i. Long Term - Increase in number of successful SAL quota attainment FY23 vs FY24

ii. Long Term - Increase in cARR YoY (overall and by Sales Leader & SAL)

iii. Long Term - Pareto analysis of SALs becoming more equal (moving away from something that would show 20% of SALs contributing 80% of cARR growth YoY)

iv. Long Term - Account Penetration and average deal size growth per territory (compare those accounts that have moved vs those that remained stable)

v. Medium Term - Analysis of account cARR growth - were the accounts moved showing higher than average cARR growth than those that stayed with the same SAL

vi. Short Term - Improvements in PipeGen and conversion rates, specifically in this case with prospects

vii. Ongoing - Feedback from Sales Leaders

viii. Ongoing - Feedback from Customer & Partners
